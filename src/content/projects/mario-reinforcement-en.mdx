## What it's about

An RL project built from scratch: an agent learns to play the NES version of Super Mario Bros purely from the pixels of the game screen. It sees the same screen a human does and learns on its own to run, jump and dodge enemies. Around it sits the full tooling so the training runs reproducibly and the result is showable in the browser.

## Milestone: Worlds 1–3 complete

All **12 levels across Worlds 1 to 3** are solved. Every level meets the same, up-front success criterion: **20 of 20 greedy episodes reaching the flag**.

- **Level 1-1** via a **hand-implemented Double DQN** (online/target network, experience replay, epsilon-greedy)
- **All remaining levels** via **PPO** (Stable-Baselines3)
- For the hard-exploration spot in **2-1**, an additional **Go-Explore savestate search**

**[→ Try the live demo in your browser](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Spl4sH/mario-reinforcement)** (Hugging Face Space, incl. Grad-CAM overlay): a clickable link where the trained agent plays in the browser.

## How it works

- **Input:** 4 stacked grayscale frames (84×84)
- **Network:** a CNN (3× conv + 2× FC) → Q-values / policy over 7 actions (run, jump, combos)
- **Reward:** progress to the right, coins, survival (reward shaping in `reward.py`)
- **Evaluation:** greedy runs with a best-checkpoint (`evaluate.py`)

## Made visible

- **Grad-CAM vision overlay:** shows what the CNN is "looking at" for each action
- **Vision GIFs** of the best runs, **death heatmaps** of the learning progress, and a **human-vs-AI comparison**

## The engineering around it

The part that turns an experiment into a real project:

- **Tests** (pytest) + **CI** via GitHub Actions (run without emulator/GPU)
- **Docker** + a **Kubernetes Job** (GPU request, persistent checkpoints/logs) for headless training
- Optional **Weights & Biases** experiment tracking to compare runs and hyperparameter sweeps
- A **Gradio demo app**, deployed as a public **Hugging Face Space**

## Next step

Still open is **Level 4-4** – a labyrinth with a non-linear reward signal that needs its own handling beyond the standard pipeline.

## Why this project

You only understand RL by building and debugging replay buffers, target networks and reward design yourself once. On top of that comes the part that turns an experiment into a real project: testing it reproducibly, containerizing it, and making it showable as a browser demo in the end.
