## What it's about

An RL project built from scratch: an agent learns to play the NES version of Super Mario Bros purely from the pixels of the game screen. Nobody tells it *how* to play – only that going right is good and dying is bad. Around it sits the full tooling so the training runs reproducibly and the result is showable in the browser.

## Result: Worlds 1–4 complete – 16 of 16 levels solved

Every level meets the same, up-front success criterion: **20 of 20 greedy episodes reaching the flag**.

- **Level 1-1** via a **hand-implemented Double DQN** (online/target network, experience replay, epsilon-greedy)
- **All remaining levels** via **PPO** (Stable-Baselines3) – the standard pipeline built up along the way (VecNormalize, LR decay, 16 parallel envs) ended up solving a new level in ~40 minutes of training
- For the two spots where pure RL failed (the trampoline tower in 2-1, the labyrinth in 4-4): **Go-Explore savestate search + behavior cloning**
- All 16 trained models published as [Release v1.0](https://github.com/Spla4sH/mario-reinforcement/releases/tag/v1.0)

**[→ Try the live demo in your browser](https://huggingface.co/spaces/Spl4sH/mario-reinforcement)** (Hugging Face Space, incl. Grad-CAM overlay)

## The most instructive stretch: reward hacking in the labyrinth

Level 4-4 gamed the reward **twice**:

1. **Loop farming:** wrong paths throw Mario back, and large x-position jumps backwards cost no reward – so the agent ran in circles forever, collecting 13,824 reward at a 0% flag rate. Fix: a `ProgressReward` wrapper that only rewards *new* x-maxima.
2. **16-bit overflow:** next, the agent farmed an emulator glitch where `x_pos` briefly jumps to 65535 on screen transitions – 57k reward for an integer overflow. Fix: only count physically plausible steps.

Both fixes were measured against the exploiting model up front, before investing new compute. The path through the labyrinth was finally found by a **multi-stage savestate search**, whose solution sequence was cloned into the policy via behavior cloning – verified with a greedy run every epoch, not by imitation accuracy.

## Made visible

- **Grad-CAM vision overlay:** shows what the CNN is "looking at" for each action – including a **DQN-vs-PPO comparison**: two algorithms, two ways of looking at the same level
- **Death heatmaps:** death positions from over 5,400 training episodes drawn over a level panorama – you can watch the level being "conquered" from left to right
- **Human vs. AI:** record your own run and race the agent as a side-by-side GIF, on any of the 16 levels

## The engineering around it

- **Tests** (pytest) + **CI** via GitHub Actions (run without emulator/GPU)
- **Docker** + **Kubernetes**: a hyperparameter sweep actually ran as **four parallel K8s Jobs** in a kind cluster – including the lessons behind it (build context, OOMKilled by the replay buffer)
- Optional **Weights & Biases** experiment tracking
- A **Gradio demo app**, deployed as a public **Hugging Face Space** with all 16 levels

## Why this project

You only understand RL by building and debugging replay buffers, target networks and reward design yourself once. Every level exhibited a different RL "disease" with its own remedy: instability → reward normalization, premature convergence → more entropy, hard exploration → savestate search, reward hacking → measurable reward fixes. On top of that comes the part that turns an experiment into a real project: testing it reproducibly, containerizing it, and making it showable as a browser demo.
